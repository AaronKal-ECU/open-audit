Executive summary

Overview

The practice sees around 600 patients a week with four doctors, one nurse and three reception and admin staff. The owner's concern is that admin staff spend most of the day on static, repetitive paperwork rather than patient-facing work. The session walked through five repetitive jobs; this audit quantifies the three strongest candidates and treats the rest as supporting context or a next-phase item.

Strengths

The practice already runs Best Practice for clinical and billing, HotDoc for online bookings and Outlook for email, so the core systems for structured intake and outbound messaging are in place. The owner has a clear view of which work is most static and repetitive.

Primary constraint

Reception and admin time is consumed by retyping paper into systems and by manual outbound contact, with tracking held in a spreadsheet that is fragile when staff are away.

Business context

Wade Court Medical Centre is a single family practice. The owner and principal GP sets direction; three reception and admin staff carry the repetitive paperwork; the practice manager runs the monthly care plan due report. Best Practice holds clinical and billing records, HotDoc handles online bookings, and Outlook carries email.

Where manual effort is concentrated

Paper intake retyped by hand

About 10 minutes typing plus 5 minutes records request per new patient, around 20 a week
New patients complete a paper form at the desk and reception types the whole thing into Best Practice, then requests previous records separately, so reception time goes into retyping rather than patient-facing work and incomplete forms push questions back into the consult.
Addressed by: New patient registration and records request

Dictation retyped into letters

About 6 minutes per letter, around 40 a week
Doctors dictate referrals and reception types each letter before sending, so a large, steady volume of typing sits with reception and delays outgoing correspondence.
Addressed by: Outgoing referral letters

Manual recall calls and fragile tracking

About 5 minutes per call, around 30 calls a week, about a third needing a second call
Reception phones every flagged patient and tracks outstanding recalls in a spreadsheet, so repeat calls consume time and tracking breaks down when someone is on leave.
Addressed by: Results recall calls

Care plan recalls reach only half of those due

About 4 hours a month
The practice manager runs a monthly due report and rings or writes to patients manually, so half of due patients are not reached, leaving chronic care reviews incomplete.
Addressed by: Results recall calls

Medicare rejection rework

About 10 minutes per rejection, around 10 a week
Rejections usually come from an expired card or a wrong item number and are fixed and resubmitted by hand, so recurring rework adds to the admin load and can be reduced by cleaner intake data.
Addressed by: New patient registration and records request

What's next

Online telehealth booking

What was asked

Patients keep asking to book telehealth online instead of ringing; whether it is worth doing now.

Position

The following item was considered and assessed in this session. The position below is a considered recommendation, not a dismissal. It is a viable next-phase opportunity that should follow the three quantified workflows, because it depends on the same online booking and patient messaging foundations.

Rationale

  • The session treated telehealth booking as a separate item rather than part of the admin paperwork audit.
  • It builds on HotDoc online booking and the patient messaging used in results recall.
  • No handling time or volume for telehealth booking was discussed, so it cannot be quantified here.

Revisit conditions

  • After the results recall and new patient intake changes are in place
  • When the practice can share telehealth request volumes and current phone handling time
