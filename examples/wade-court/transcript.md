# Discovery call: Wade Court Medical Centre (synthetic)

> Demonstration material for OpenAudit. Wade Court Medical Centre is a family practice that has since closed, and the name is used as a tribute. This conversation and every figure in it are fictional.

**Format:** video call, 35 minutes
**Attendees:** Aaron Kalaji (automation consultant), Dr Marhaf Kalaji (owner and principal GP)

---

**Aaron:** Thanks for making the time. I want to understand where your admin team's week actually goes, so we can pick the three automations worth doing first.

**Dr Kalaji:** Good. Honestly, that's the problem. I've got three good people on reception and they spend most of the day on the same paperwork over and over. They're stuck doing static admin when they should be looking after patients at the front desk.

**Aaron:** What does the practice look like day to day?

**Dr Kalaji:** Four doctors, one nurse, three reception and admin staff. We see around 600 patients a week. We use Best Practice for clinical and billing, HotDoc for online bookings, Outlook for email.

**Aaron:** Let's go through the repetitive jobs. Where would you start?

**Dr Kalaji:** New patients. We get about 20 a week. They fill in a paper form at the desk and one of the girls types the whole thing into Best Practice. About ten minutes a form. Then she requests their old records from the previous clinic, another five minutes.

**Aaron:** And if the form's incomplete?

**Dr Kalaji:** Then I'm asking the same questions again in the consult. Allergies, medications, the usual.

**Aaron:** What's next on the list?

**Dr Kalaji:** Recalls. When a doctor marks a result as needing a follow-up appointment, reception has to ring the patient and book them in. About 30 calls a week, five minutes each. Maybe a third don't pick up, so they call again the next day.

**Aaron:** How do you keep track of the ones who never get through?

**Dr Kalaji:** A spreadsheet. It works until someone's on leave.

**Aaron:** Anything recurring like care plan reviews?

**Dr Kalaji:** Yes. Patients on chronic care plans are due a review about every six months. Once a month the practice manager runs a report and rings or writes to everyone who's due. Takes her about four hours a month, and she reaches maybe half of them.

**Aaron:** And outgoing paperwork, referral letters?

**Dr Kalaji:** We dictate them and reception types them up and sends them. About 40 a week, six minutes each.

**Aaron:** Billing?

**Dr Kalaji:** Medicare rejections. Ten or so a week come back, usually an expired card or a wrong item number. Ten minutes each to fix and resubmit.

**Aaron:** If you could take one thing off their plate tomorrow, what would it be?

**Dr Kalaji:** The typing. Forms and letters. It's the most static, most repetitive part of the job.

**Aaron:** Any bigger questions you want us to look at?

**Dr Kalaji:** Patients keep asking to book telehealth online instead of ringing. I'd like to know if that's worth doing now.

**Aaron:** We'll treat that as a separate item. Last thing: roughly what does an hour of admin time cost you with on-costs?

**Dr Kalaji:** About 38 dollars.

**Aaron:** Perfect. We'll put the audit together and come back to you.
